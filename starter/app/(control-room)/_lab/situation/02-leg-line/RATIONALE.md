# situation 02: Leg line

- References: Flighty's flight rows, "SFO 09:10 → JFK 17:25" with the state in words (https://flighty.com, shot `research/premium/visual/shots/map-05-flighty-live-map.png`); Fastship shipment details, origin and destination on one line with a connector between (https://dribbble.com/shots/25270929-Fastship-Shipment-Details-Dashboard, `research/premium/visual/shots/situation-01-fastship-shipment-details.png`).
- Borrowed: the leg as one horizontal line, A on the left and B on the right, the distance on the line, each end's place and time under its letter; the line keeps Bahn's orange-to-ink route colours.
- For the ops person: "where from, where to, how far, when" reads in one glance without a map, which loads late and says little at this size (the Ferrari's ends are 0.3 km apart). The map is one row away ("Show on the map").
- Lowest cognitive load of the set: four lines of facts, one optional map, the order link.
- Trade-off: no map by default, so a dispatcher who reads geography first (which side of the river, which motorway) needs one more tap; two long place names share one line's width.
