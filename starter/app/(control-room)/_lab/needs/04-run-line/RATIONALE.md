# needs 04: Where you are, as one line

Cut by the reviewer (RV1, 25 Oct): the marks carry no words until pointed at, red marks need a legend to read, the name tag covers the headline, and at 37 tasks a mark is a sliver; it also keeps today's Other tasks row, so it adds a line without solving the many-tasks list.

Borrows from GitHub's pull request review progress (https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/collaborating-with-pull-requests/reviewing-changes-in-pull-requests/reviewing-proposed-changes-in-a-pull-request), HEY Focus & Reply (https://www.hey.com/features/reply-mode/) and Eugenia Ho's "Queues" with its Time left order (https://dribbble.com/shots/3442387-Queues, `research/premium/visual/shots/needs-14-queues-time-left.png`).
What it takes: GitHub's quiet progress in the header (files viewed of all files) instead of a list; HEY's idea of working through one pile one after another; the queue ordered by the time left.
Here: one line above the card, one mark per task in decide-by order. The one you are on is solid and taller, marks due within the hour are a soft red, decided marks fill grey on the left; "2 of 12" at the end. Hover or focus names a mark (car, decide-by, place) and a click opens it; arrow keys, Home and End walk the marks. 3, 12 and 37 tasks are the same single line.
Why for an ops person under time pressure: the size of the queue, how much of it is urgent and where you are read in one glance, without a list taking room from the card.
Trade-off: a mark carries no words until you point at it; at 37 marks each is thin (still a 24 px tall target). Decided marks are kept for this browser tab only.
