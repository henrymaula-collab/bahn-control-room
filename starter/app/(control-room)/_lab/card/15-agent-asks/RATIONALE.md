# card 15: The agent asks

- References: Zendesk auto assist (https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/9945148867866-About-auto-assist, `research/premium/product/shots/card-03-zendesk-auto-assist-approve.png`): the assistant's proposal in its own named box, the person's Edit and Approve under it; Claude in Chrome's plan approval (`research/premium/product/shots/chat-05-claude-chrome-plan-approval.png`): the agent's plan, then the person's answers.
- Borrowed: a byline with Bahn's mark (the same mark the chat uses for the agent) and one sentence that carries the deadline: "The agent stopped here and needs a decision by 13:00", the time in deadline red. The situation and the question sit behind a thin ink rule as the agent's note; the answers sit outside it, as the person's.
- Why for ops: it makes the product's promise visible on the one screen the video shows: the agent did the legwork and stopped, a person decides. The deadline reads as a request from someone, not a label.
- Trade-off: the byline is one more line before the situation, and the rule indents the headline by about 20 px, so the longest headline may take a fourth line.
