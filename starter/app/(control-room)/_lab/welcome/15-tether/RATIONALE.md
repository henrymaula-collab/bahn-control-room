# welcome 15: Rows tied to their cars

- References: Flighty's live map, where the flight in the list and its plane on the map are one object (https://flighty.com, `research/premium/visual/shots/map-05-flighty-live-map.png`); FleetFlow's live map, one red marker with a halo as the "look here" signal (https://dribbble.com, `research/premium/visual/shots/map-06-fleetflow-live-map.png`); the map board's finding that one accent line on a grey map is the strongest pointer (`research/premium/visual/principles.md` 6).
- Borrowed: the row and the dot as one thing, drawn: a thin ink hairline leaves the panel's edge at the row and curves to the car's red pin, with a ring around the pin.
- By default the most urgent task is tied (the BMW M4 in Helsinki); hovering or tabbing to any row moves the line to that car and darkens it; it follows the map when it pans or zooms, and hides when the pin is off screen or behind the panel.
- Why for ops: the panel and the map stop being two separate pictures; Aino sees where her first car is without searching the map for a red dot among 26.
- Trade-off: a line across the map is new visual furniture; it stays thin and single, never one per row.
