# menu 01: Your work, then Operations

References: Apple HIG Sidebars (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sidebars, `research/premium/visual/shots/menu-mm-hig-sidebars.png`: "Section" headers over the rows); Linear's sidebar, the person's rows first, then "Your teams" (https://nicelydone.club/examples/screens/7434ce1f-0ec6-45cb-9a44-10269b2e55c2, `research/premium/visual/shots/menu-03-linear-sidebar.png`); Lightfield's grey section labels (https://nicelydone.club/examples/screens/ecc513fb-cb1e-4733-aaff-30c5ea0cb251, `research/premium/visual/shots/menu-07-lightfield-sidebar.png`).

Borrowed: two groups with one small grey header in sentence case (never caps), the person's own destinations first without a header, the shared records under one.

Why it helps: seven equal rows make Orders look as urgent as Your tasks. Split in two, the top three are "my shift" (where I look, what I decide, what I decided) and the rest are the room's records an ops person opens on demand. "Operations" is Bahn's word (the Operations board, the Operations Associate title).

Trade-off: one more line of text in the menu, and the header must stay true if items are added.
