# decide 06: Send and next

**Reference**: Zendesk Play mode and its Submit menu ("Next available ticket" or "Stay on ticket"), https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/9186492658714-Using-Play-mode-to-quickly-work-through-tickets, shot `research/premium/product/shots/needs-07-zendesk-submit-next.png` (board `product/needs.md` 05, `product/decide.md` 03); Superhuman "Achieve Inbox Zero" and Mark Done with Z to undo, https://help.superhuman.com/hc/en-us/articles/46005833597709-Achieve-Inbox-Zero, shot `research/premium/product/shots/needs-05-superhuman-get-me-to-zero.png` (board `product/needs.md` 01 and 03).

**What it borrows**: Zendesk's split submit: the button says what happens after it, with "Send and stay" one tap away in a small menu, remembered next time; Superhuman's move: the next case opens the moment the person acts, and the undo travels with them. A line over the buttons says where Send goes next ("Then Volvo V60, Velantora opens"). The toast at the foot of the window, "Email to Kovellin Automobilhandel goes in 7 s, Undo", stays on the next case; Undo (or Z) cancels it and brings the email back on its own case.

**Why it helps**: an ops person with sixteen cases never waits on a receipt; the next decision is on screen within a fifth of a second. Staying is still there for the one case where you want to watch the agent call the branch. On a case you stayed on, the next one opens by itself in 5 s, with Stay here.

**Trade-off**: moving on at once means the person does not see the agent's carry-out; the toast needs its own small root on the page to survive the move.
