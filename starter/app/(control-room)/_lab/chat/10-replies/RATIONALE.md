# chat 10: The yes is a reply

- References: Hark's short confirms, "Want me to post this?" / "yes" / "Posted to #product-launch." (https://nicelydone.club/examples/screens/93b5ead1-f484-4f77-9625-a77409b86e85, `research/premium/visual/shots/chat-05-hark-short-confirms.png`); Apple Messages' suggested actions under a message (https://www.apple.com/apple-intelligence/, `research/premium/chat-builder/shots/apple-messages-suggestions.jpg`); Mutiny's "Done:" result (https://nicelydone.club/examples/screens/b863a3c4-c19d-4e69-beec-19e32c2db5dc, `research/premium/visual/shots/chat-06-mutiny-done-what-it-will-do.png`).
- Borrowed: the carry-out loop as a short exchange (Hark), the actions as replies under the message (Apple), the result that leads with what happened (Mutiny); Hark's quiet status line above the field while it works.
- Exactly: under the agent's offer, "Send the email" (ink) and "Not now" (outlined) sit on the person's side; the tap becomes the person's words in the thread and the agent answers in one sentence: "Decided at 12:04. The agent checks with the branch again at 10:00 ..."; "Not now" gets "Nothing was sent. The card still waits for your decision." The agent's messages have the mark at the side, no bubble.
- Why for ops: the decision reads as part of the conversation that led to it, and the confirmation is one sentence with the time, which is what an ops person checks.
- Trade-off: the replies look like the person's own words before they are said; a conversational yes is less formal than an approval card (variant 06) for a step that writes to a customer.
