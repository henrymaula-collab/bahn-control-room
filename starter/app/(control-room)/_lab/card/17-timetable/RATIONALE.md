# card 17: Timetable

- References: Apple HIG Live Activities (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities, raw `research/premium/product/raw/pp/hig-live-activities.md`): for a task with "a defined beginning and end", show what people need "at a glance". Flighty's times as the organising column (https://flighty.com, shot `research/premium/visual/shots/card-13-flighty-was-now.png`). A queue where time left is its own column (shot `research/premium/visual/shots/needs-14-queues-time-left.png`).
- Borrowed: the deadline organises the card. A time column runs down the left like a line on a timetable: "12:00 Now" beside the situation, "13:00 in 60 min" beside the question and the answer, "Then" beside what follows. The hour between now and the deadline is the line itself, ink to the map's red within the hour (the same gradient as the route on the map); what follows is dotted. The minutes count down while the card is open.
- Why for ops: "Decide by 13:00" asks for arithmetic against the clock; "12:00 now, 13:00 in 60 min" does it, and the order (now, your answer, then) is the order the person thinks in. Bahn is a railway word; a timetable is the industry's own picture.
- Trade-off: the time column takes about five characters of width, so the situation steps down a little to keep three lines; a deadline days away shows its day in the column.
