# rule 02: The rule as a setting with a switch

- Reference: Apple Shortcuts personal automation (`research/premium/rule/shots/rule-02-apple-shortcuts-automation.png`): an automation is When plus Do, and one setting decides whether it runs on its own or asks first. Vercel Deployment Protection (https://vercel.com/docs/deployment-protection, `research/premium/rule/shots/rule-05-vercel-deployment-protection.png`) and Linear workflow automations (`research/premium/rule/shots/rule-07-linear-automations.png`): protection and automations are per-project toggles.
- Borrowed: an iOS inset grouped list (When, Then, then a switch row "The agent does this alone" with "Off. A person decides each time." under it) and a Settings footnote under the group that says who taught it and how to take it back. The switch is a real `role="switch"` (Space or Enter), 200 ms.
- Why for ops: it says what it is, a standing setting, not a one-off case to clear; the same switch is how Kaisa takes it back, so on and off are one gesture in one place.
- The answers behind it are one row down: the four people with "9 of 9 the same", Sofie's one different answer, History.
- Trade-off: a switch invites flipping; there is no confirm step (Apple: switches act at once), so the footnote carries the consequence.
