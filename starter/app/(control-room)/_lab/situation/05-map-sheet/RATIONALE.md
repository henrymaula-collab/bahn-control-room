# situation 05: Map sheet

- References: Apple Human Interface Guidelines, Maps, place cards ("Keep the location on your map visible when displaying a place card"; on iPhone the card is a sheet from the bottom edge), https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/maps (`research/premium/situation-si/shots/hig-maps-place-card-iphone-full.png`); Nexroute order history, a white card floating over a full map (https://dribbble.com/shots/27502985-Logistics-Dashboard-Shipment-Details-Route-Tracking-UI, `research/premium/visual/shots/situation-06-nexroute-order-history.png`).
- Borrowed: the map as the whole panel, the facts on a sheet with a grabber at its foot; the grabber folds the sheet to the car and the blocker so the map gets the room.
- For the ops person: the most "Find My" of the set: where the car is and where it goes is the background, and the facts stay one glance away on the sheet. It makes the panel feel like a live place, not a form.
- Trade-off: the most map, so the most colour and movement next to the card; it competes more than the others with the question. The sheet covers the map's foot, so the map pads its fit to keep both pins above it.
