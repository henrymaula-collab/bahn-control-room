# map 07: Paper map, fading under the panel

References: Apple HIG Maps, the muted style (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/maps, `research/premium/product/shots/map-01b-apple-hig-muted-style.png`); TravelPerk's quiet map with a small panel (https://nicelydone.club/examples/screens/5ebb1da8-33ec-4545-a29e-c52efe34b9d8, `research/premium/visual/shots/map-01-travelperk-nobody-traveling.png`); Airbnb's muted base with few strong marks (https://nicelydone.club/examples/screens/3ffcf422-3651-4e7e-8947-e73b295d2080, `research/premium/visual/shots/map-02-airbnb-pins-in-map-area.png`).

Borrowed: Apple's "desaturated version of the map ... if you have a lot of information-rich content that you want to stand out", here in Bahn's paper tones (no green parks, grey-teal water, lighter labels); the panel sitting on a calm ground; red dots lifted by a soft shadow instead of a halo.

Why it helps: the cars become the only colour on screen, and the map fades to paper under the welcome panel, so the frame reads as "panel, then the cars beside it" instead of a map that happens to be covered.

Trade-off: water and land are close in tone, so geography is a little harder to read at a glance; the fade hides the UK and the North Sea, which hold no cars anyway.
